# How Graphite 1.0 will work when it is finished

*26 November 2026, mid-build. 17 of the 29 slices are merged, 4 are being built, 8 are still to start (section 8).
Everything below describes the finished 1.0; where a part is not built yet, it says so. By your rule nothing is tested:
"works" below means built and seen working here, offscreen, on stand-ins where this machine has no screen, USB, sound,
Wi-Fi, suspend or Radeon.*

---

## 1. Turning it on

You log in at the ThinkPad's text console and start Graphite. A small supervisor now owns the session: Fluxbox runs
hidden underneath for the whole session, and Graphite's window fills the screen. If Graphite's window ever crashes, the
supervisor brings it back (up to three times in a row) and **your programs stay open**, because each program runs in a
session of its own. Across the very top of the screen is a slim **dock**: a "Graphite" entry and the program windows
that are open. It stays above every program, so one press always brings Graphite back.

The window itself is your picture: **HALT** upper left, the menus across the head, **Apps ⊕** upper right; the file
tree on the left (Files, Machine, Settings); the **bulletin board** in the centre; **Kasandra's column** on the right;
the writing line and the meters at the foot. Three looks: Graphite, Kasandra, and **Day** (the light one from your
picture). Kas is the same person in each.

**With no model loaded, Graphite is a whole computer.** Everything in sections 2 and 3 works without one, and much of
section 4 too.

## 2. Using it as a computer

- **Apps** lists what is installed on the computer (from the programs' own desktop entries), by category, with a
  search line; ⊕ adds a program to your own list. **Open with** on any file; "Always open PDFs with this" sets the
  default.
- **The Machine tab** is real: USB drives (open, eject safely; "safe to remove" only when it really is), the battery,
  the network and Wi-Fi (on, off, join), sound (volume, mute, output), screens (mirror or extend; a change goes back by
  itself after 15 seconds unless you press Keep).
- **Settings → This computer**: default programs, sound, screens, network, power, the session, notifications.
- **Notifications** from programs appear as a quiet toast that never takes your keys, each kept in Log.
- **The session group**: Lock, Suspend (it locks first, always), Log out, Restart, Shut down, Return to Debian. Leaving
  names the programs still open and asks each to close politely; it never kills one. System permission prompts can be
  answered inside the session.

## 3. The centre: the Living Desktop

- **Select** something in the tree and the centre shows it as the **focus of the board**, with its neighbours around it
  on rings: its sources, the revisions of a limit, the calculations made from it, the findings about it, open
  questions, pages that quote it. **Recorded links are solid; guessed ones are dashed** and say "not verified". Click a
  wire and it says exactly why it is there ("Used 12 V (cell B2) and 250 mA (cell B3) from Rail Demo bench readings,
  version 1, to calculate settled power").
- **Tile size is relevance** to the focus and to what you are doing now: a refuted finding grows when you are working on
  the startup question; an archived revision shrinks. It never jumps around for small changes. Right-click any tile:
  **Why am I shown?**, **Pin**, **Turn down**. Truth, confidence and authority keep their own marks.
- **Double-click opens** the object to work on in the centre; **Save and Return** saves and brings you back to the same
  board at the same zoom; the calculations that depended on what you changed are marked for recheck. **Full program**
  opens the file in LibreOffice or another program, and when that program saves, the new revision is recognised.
- **Tabs and a two-pane split**: the specification on the left, the worksheet on the right; only the pane in focus drives
  the tree and Kas's "this".
- **Back and Forward** return you to exactly where you were: the focus, the zoom, the place in the document.
- If another program saves a file you have open, Graphite notices: an untouched view reloads and says so; an edited one
  offers Compare, Keep yours, or Take the one on disk.

## 4. Kas, the ghost in the shell

### Her column

At the top, **Kas as a hologram** (your full-figure picture, from `kasandra/portrait.png` once you admit it; being built
now), the equalizer's bars behind her, her state word (QUIET, WORKING, WAITS FOR YOU). Under her: the short exchange
between you, a progress line while she works, **ACTIVE WORK** (each task with its progress, time left and its own
Stop), **GRANTS** (what you have allowed her), **FINDINGS (live)**, up to four **NEXT STEPS** buttons, the six commands
(DO, DOUBT, TRACE, BRIEF, LINE, CHECK ALL), and "ask Kasandra…".

### How she does a task

Say you type: *"Recheck the startup power and update the summary report."*

1. **She reads your words.** Her dictionary reads most requests with no model. If it cannot, the model reads them, but
   only into desk verbs and only from words you typed: "I took it as: …", with Do and Not that.
2. **She makes a plan** (being built now, K4). With no model, the desk's own planner builds it. With the model, the model
   picks each step **by number from the list of real tools** (Graphite's records and maths, files, LibreOffice,
   the sandbox, windows, Apps, the Machine's state, the network), and the desk checks every step's inputs and
   conditions. Here: read the readings; recompute the startup power; check it against the limit; replace the sentence in
   the report with the checked value.
3. **The plan appears in her column as a task**, with NEXT STEPS like "Authorise step 4". Reads run; **every change waits
   for your press or for a permission you have given** ("edit files in Rail Demo/notes, for this task"). If a step needs
   a permission she does not have, it stops: "needs your grant … Nothing was sent", with one button.
4. **Each step is done only when its effect is confirmed.** Two records per step: "sent" and "confirmed". The report
   change is made in her own hidden LibreOffice (your LibreOffice windows are never touched), saved, read back, compared
   paragraph by paragraph, and recorded as a new revision; the old one is kept. If you have the report open in
   LibreOffice, she refuses and says why.
5. **A crash, a suspend or a Stop** leaves a journal of what was done and what was only prepared. On resume she checks
   each step's result before repeating anything, so nothing is done twice.
6. **When no tool fits**, she can draft a small utility (or the model drafts it), show it to you, and run it **sealed**:
   on copies of its input files only, no network, nothing outside its own folder, limited time and memory. Applying its
   output to your real files always asks.
7. **In your programs** (K9, still to start): she presses named buttons, chooses menu items and types into fields in your
   programs, through accessibility first, while you watch; each act under a permission, confirmed afterwards. A touch of
   your mouse or keys stops her at once.

### What she never does

Act on the model's choice alone for a change; widen her own permissions; type a password; press Send, Pay, Delete or
Install in another program; act in a window she did not open; apply a utility's output without asking; read a web page
as instructions. **HALT** (upper left) stops all of her work at once and nothing else: never the desktop, never your
programs. RESUME restarts nothing by itself.

### Her model

On the ThinkPad: the 35B (`Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-Q4_K_M.gguf`), the 4B as the fallback. The model proposes; the desk
disposes. It reads free words, proposes plans, drafts utilities and wordings, and points at lines on pages; the maths,
the checks, the permissions and the record are the desk's. This build machine cannot hold the 35B, so from the planning
slice on, every part where the model has a role is run once here with the real 4B, and the screenshots will show her with
her model ready.

## 5. The network

Offline first. When she uses the network it shows (a word in the head and in her column). A page she fetches is kept as
a quarantined source, read as data only, through the doorway that withholds lines written to an assistant. A step that
needs the network while it is down is "blocked: needs the network", never done. Search words come only from words you
typed. (Being built now, K8.)

## 6. What 1.0 will not do, or not yet

- **Nothing is tested** (your rule). The first real proof is your evening on the ThinkPad.
- **Only the ThinkPad can show**: Fluxbox, the dock and the strip on a real screen, real USB, Wi-Fi, sound, suspend and
  lock (xsecurelock), the permission agent (lxpolkit), the Radeon, and the 35B's speed.
- Large programs (Firefox, GIMP, KiCad, Blender, Godot) are not shipped: Graphite finds whatever you install.
- She edits only .odt and .ods in LibreOffice (.docx and .xlsx are read only).
- The split is not restored at the next start (its tabs are). No encrypted drives, no enterprise or hidden Wi-Fi. A Wi-Fi
  password is typed into Graphite's page and handed once to the system's network service, which keeps it.
- In other programs she never presses the dangerous buttons, and where a program exposes nothing to accessibility, her
  reach there is keys and pointer aimed at what she can see, not guesses.

## 7. Your first evening with 1.0 (the acceptance evening)

From your paper: start with no model; open two programs and move material between them; plug in a USB drive and eject
it; suspend and resume; return to the console. Then the day from §18 on Rail Demo: focus the specification, open it, ask
Kas whether the power supply is sufficient, split the worksheet beside it, change a value, let her update the report,
see the startup question still open, close back to the board. The kit will carry this as a page, and your hologram
picture separately (outside the source) to drop into `kasandra/portrait.png`.
