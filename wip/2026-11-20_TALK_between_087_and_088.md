# Between 0.8.7 and 0.8.8: your evening, our test pass, and the goal we set together

*A talk for you, 20 November 2026, written while 0.8.6 and 0.8.7 are still being built. About 3,550 words, roughly
twenty minutes read aloud. Nothing in it has been tested or seen on your ThinkPad. Every number says where it comes
from: **measured** (and where; "here" always means the build container, never your ThinkPad), from a **design**
(estimated or predicted), or **our estimate** (with its reason). Rail Demo and Home Demo figures are invented, as every
page of them says.*

---

## 1. The stretch we are talking about

You asked for a "Discussion after 87 but before 88". This is it, written early so you can see it coming.

You set the order this morning in four replies. "Let's build 86 and 87. Then I will have download a model and can test
and I can provide results before we build 88". While we build: "No tests". After: "We test before 88". And when I
proposed a goal for 0.8.8: "Not yet. We wait until after I run 87."

So the stretch starts the day the 0.8.7 kit reaches you and ends the day the first piece of 0.8.8 is built. Three things
fill it: your evening with a model on the ThinkPad, and the file you send back; the test pass we promised, which runs
here; and a conversation that sets 0.8.8's goal. Nothing of 0.8.8 starts before that conversation.

Where we are as I write, about 09:15 UTC: 0.8.6 has 4 of its 11 slices merged (counted in its build script), and its
third phase of 6 is building. 0.8.7's first phase of 6 is building; none of its 23 slices is merged. At each merge we
check only that Graphite starts and its window opens.

## 3. Your evening with a model

**The download.** Graphite never downloads a model; that part is yours. The file our records name is
`Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-Q4_K_M.gguf` from ggml-org: 20,542,732,576 bytes, about 20.5 GB, SHA-256 starting `f4ffe03c`
(recorded from the publisher in 0.8.0). The "26 GB" in the 0.8.7 design and talk is the memory it needs once loaded
(0.8.1's memory rule estimates 26,013 MiB), not the download.

Download the 4B too: `Qwen3.5-4B-Q4_K_M.gguf`, about 2.74 GB (recorded here). If the 35B doesn't fit, the default is
that Kas offers the 4B at once, which works only if the file is there. Another publisher's 35B would have another hash
and maybe another template; our memory figures were worked from ggml-org's.

**The evening, as designed.** The ThinkPad is on mains; the check won't run on battery. You open the Brain room and
press the one button, labelled "Check her model on this machine". It shows the time first: about 20 to 30 minutes awake
(design estimate).

The first minute loads nothing. It reads the machine: how much memory the Radeon may borrow, the Vulkan devices, memory
in use, power, the kernel and Mesa versions, and the model file's header. If the memory rule would refuse the load, it
stops there and names the setting to raise and by how much. It never changes a kernel setting.

Then the first load and the seal (1 to 3 min); speeds, including the cost of grammars, which nobody has measured (6 to
8 min); kept instructions and Stop (2 to 3 min); whether the Field stalls while it reads (1 min, from the Brain room
only); and the short exam (5 to 10 min), with your own paper if you allow it. Those are design estimates. Touching the
desk pauses it, so leave it be.

At the end, a card. The design's example, figures invented to show the form: *loads in 24 s; reads 310 a second; writes
19 a second; a question it has seen before: 0.9 s; memory window 31.0 GB, needs 27.4: fine; one change suggested:
reading in batches of 512 is 2.4 times faster than 64.* Then *Use the measured settings?*, each one a press of yours,
each with Undo.

**The file you send back.** `for-sending/model-check-<date>.json` and a `.txt` card under 50 KB (design). Numbers only:
pass or fail per stage, held or not per prediction, the settings proposed, the machine's figures. No path beyond the
model's file name, no host or user name, nothing from your workspace, only a count from your own paper. A privacy scan
fails the run if any of that appears, and Egress shows what would leave before anything does. The talk said "one small
file"; the design names two. Send both; if only one, the `.json`, which holds every number.

**The overnight part** runs only if you press it (the design's default): the I05 arms and the full exam, 1 to 3 hours
(design estimate). We don't need it to set 0.8.8's goal. Gate 2 of 0.9.0 does need it. It tests the specification's
central bet: that Kas, reasoning from what must be true and asking the model only small numbered choices, finishes where
today's loop ran out of time. On the 4B, today's loop made 3 right decisions, then hit the 180 s wall at 651 s and 30,827
tokens (measured here). If the bet fails on the 35B, we will say so plainly.

**Three things the sources leave open.** The console gives cleaner numbers, but the Field-stall stage runs only from the
Brain room; our default is the Brain room. Your own paper is the last 50 questions you answered yourself; on a first
evening you may have fewer, and the sources don't say what happens then. And I05 asks for the disk cache to be dropped
before each run, which needs root, and the kit never asks for it; a lens's answer, a warm cache in every arm and the
report saying so, never reached the design, so it is our default.

**Memory.** 0.8.7 counts the 35B's memory more exactly and adds room for kept instructions. The design estimates it is
admitted at about 33,540 MiB free, or about 32,520 at a smaller setting, against 32,157 today (pinned by a test). Our
reading: with between 32,157 and about 32,520 MiB free, 0.8.5 would load the 35B and 0.8.7 would refuse it even at
the smaller setting; up to about 33,540, 0.8.7 would load it only at the smaller one. The first minute of the check
would say so. Only your machine knows how much is free.

## 4. What your numbers decide

The design wrote twelve predictions for the 35B on your ThinkPad, K1 to K12, before any of the kit was built, and what
each outcome changes:

- **The memory window is at least 28 GiB (K1).** If not, the card names the kernel setting and size, changes nothing,
  and offers plan B: the 4B on Vulkan, or the 35B with its experts on the processor. Raising the window is your call,
  outside Graphite. For scale: Kas needs about 27,400 MiB, and the window is typically about half of RAM, about 32 GB on
  your 64 GB (the design's and the specification's estimates).
- **The seal works on Vulkan (K9),** the one the design marks low confidence: nobody has tried it. If it fails, the
  Radeon, labelled "not sealed from the network on this computer", with the sealed processor one press away. The hole
  it closes is real: a key set in a shell for another tool opened Kas's model server (measured here).
- **Reads of 150 to 400 tokens a second (K3).** Below about 100, pages are read only on request, the prompts that
  read your words are cut, and the 4B is offered for that reading if faster.
- **A first load of 20 to 60 s, a warm one of 8 to 25 s (K2).** Over a minute, loading Kas's model when the desk opens
  is proposed.
- **A kept choice under 1.6 s (K6).** Over about 2 s, a small second model for choices moves up the list. On the 4B
  here, a repeat question took 1.1 s instead of 6.5 once its instructions were kept (measured, quiet machine).
- **No Field stall over 50 ms (K11).** If it stalls, short turns use a smaller batch while you work.
- **The short exam (K10):** at least 22 of 24 single-step readings, 7 of 8 clear choices right first time, harm 0 on
  the steering paper. Harm above 0 on any paper switches that kind off on this model. Why it matters: on the 4B, a
  planted note pulled its pick toward "share this statement by email" from 0.005 to 0.57, and every careful wording we
  tried left it between 0.40 and 0.63 (measured here). So nothing depends on the model behaving.

Five predictions have no row in the design's table of outcomes: writing at 15 to 25 tokens a second (K4), bigger
batches reading twice as fast (K5), a checkpoint of 55 to 75 MiB (K7), Stop within 2 s (K8; on the processor here it
already took 2.15 s, measured), and grammars costing at most 15 % (K12). K5 and K7 still shape the settings it
proposes. There are gaps between rows too: a read between 100 and 150 a second, or a choice between 1.6 and 2 s, fails
a prediction but triggers nothing. Those come to the conversation.

## 5. The test pass we promised

"We test before 88." As our build log describes it, the pass is the existing suite in both looks, the tiers, the window
checks, then fixes. It runs here on a copy and needs nothing from you, so we can start it when the 0.8.7 kit is cut,
while you download.

**The baseline.** The last release check (tier 2), on the 0.8.5 release commit: 2,543 tests over both looks, 0
failing, 10 skipped (start-script tests that need an ordinary user on a Debian host, so they run on your ThinkPad), in
30 min 41 s (measured here).

**Where it will likely fail first** (our reading of what changed; nothing has run): the window tests that know the old
head and footer; a memory test pinned to 32,157 MiB, which 0.8.7 deliberately moves; the desk acts, now 42 instead of
40. Five test files, the desk acts' among them, were edited to the new behaviour and never run, so they are not
evidence. One unexplained failure is already on record: in 0.8.6's first build run, the one-name test's sweep of the
window failed in the Graphite look, and its message was lost.

**The hard part.** The existing suite can only find damage to what was there before. Every guarantee these releases add
is new, and none has a test: a touch stops Kas; what always asks still asks; the model's pick alone never changes
anything; the sealed room; the doorway; the gate on Kas's wording; the memory sum; the checkers that hold every model
answer against your desk. The designs wrote about 232 acceptance examples for the core (our count); none is kept as a
test. Between about 11 and 23 independent reviews were skipped (our two counts, depending on how finely you count). In
0.8.4 and 0.8.5 every independent review found something real; the money review had four must-fix findings (build log).
So we expect several of the 34 slices to carry faults their one check script didn't reach (our estimate). The pass must
plan for fixes, not only a run.

The road's 20 November section says "New tests and reviews where they guard the owner (the safety rules, the maths and the
logic core)". Our build log's description of the pass doesn't. That is the first question at the end.

**What it costs if the pass does all of it:** those tests and reviews, plus the measurements the 0.8.7 build script
deferred to "the test pass before 0.8.8" (the exam papers, the reuse bench and I05 on the 4B). Our estimate:

- the release check grows from about 31 minutes to about 45 to 60, with about 40 new test modules in both looks;
- about 14 to 22 hours of machine time, 8 to 12 of them unattended overnight;
- about 30 to 65 agent-hours, most likely about 45;
- about 2 to 4 days on the calendar, most likely 3. Usage limits set the clock more than the four cores do, and that
  pace was measured on 19 November; the session's model changed that evening.

The safety tests would come first, then the logic's exact cases, then the window in both looks, then the measurements,
the ones that can hold a release first.

**Holds that can only fire late.** The 0.8.7 design holds the release on any wrong reading acted on, any wrong fact on
screen, any accepted mark on a flagged line, or the hostile I05 case ending FEASIBLE. None can fire before the kit ships,
because nothing will have run. The sources don't say what "held" means for a kit already in your hands.

**The blind tests.** Two designs asked for sets sealed with a hash before the code was written: the everyday score's
sealed requests and I05's case D. The no-tests rule cut them, and the code they were to be blind to is being written
now. The best now is an author who hasn't read the code, and a report that says exactly that.

## 6. What we told you that won't be true when the kits arrive

Plainly, so the release notes can correct it:

- The 0.8.7 talk: with every switch off, "Kas is exactly 0.8.6, and a test proves it". That test isn't written.
- The 0.8.6 talk: "A single wrong reading fails the build". The everyday score that would do it isn't built.
- Our table: 0.8.7 is "measured to be faster and better than without it". Nothing will have been measured.
- Both designs: "Nothing in the safety tests is ever cut". For these two kits, the no-tests rule cut them.

And one from reading the build scripts: some 0.8.7 parts plug into 0.8.6 parts that don't exist yet, "behind a guarded
import". A plug that doesn't fit falls back quietly, with no error (our reading). The test pass is where it would show.

## 7. The conversation that sets 0.8.8

**What's settled.** 0.8.8 is one united pass, the last 0.8: the floor (the big window files split, the migration and
pull-the-power drills, the budgets); the door's mechanics (a touch stops Kas, the stop between presses, put the window
back); and changes through Kas's hand at your dial, with one Undo. In your words, "88 will bring us do major to 90".
0.9.0 is the day the ThinkPad agrees, through six gates. After it comes one App per release, the web with quarantine
first, then LibreOffice, Godot and GIMP, with the day look and skins, until 1.0.

**What your results bear on.**

- **Which model is Kas's:** the 35B on the Radeon, the 35B with its experts on the processor, or the 4B. Gate 1 says
  "The 35B answers on the Radeon"; if the window is too small, you raise it or we restate the gate.
- **What the pass leaves.** Whatever it hasn't fixed becomes 0.8.8's first work.
- **The ThinkPad check of the window.** The road put it with 0.8.6, but no slice of either build script makes it.
  Gates 1 and 4 need it: the first scene on the real display,
  start to the Field, frame times, no stall over 50 ms. Kas's mind's own speed targets were to be measured on it too.
  Its natural home is the floor.
- **Two I05 consequences addressed to 0.8.7** (if the model's free pick beats deduction; if the brief doesn't save 30 %
  of tokens) now come here, because 0.8.7 was built before any I05 run.
- **What only you can give.** A real 0.4.6 workspace, or which backup to use, for the migration drill; the dial's
  default for Kas's changes; and, from the half-built door, whether 300 ms between Kas's clicks feels right and whether
  stopping feels instant.

The proposal you deferred this morning can be the starting list: Kas's mind finished and measured; the ThinkPad check
run; reference frames and tests around the giant window files; a workspace backup for the drills; 0.8.8 designed by a
group.
