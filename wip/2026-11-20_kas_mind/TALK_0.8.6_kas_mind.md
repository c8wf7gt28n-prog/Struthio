# Kas's mind, with no model: Graphite 0.8.6, in about fifteen minutes

*A talk for the owner, 20 November 2026. About 2,200 words. Every number here was worked out exactly. Rail Demo is the
invented rail already in the program. Home Demo is a small invented household this pass adds; its invented tenancy law
says INVENTED: NOT THE LAW OF ANY PLACE on every page. The full design is `DESIGN_0.8.6_kas_mind.md`.*

---

## 1. What this pass is for

You asked for a pass that makes Kas intelligent without a language model loaded, so that she uses one well when it is
loaded, and you asked us to be creative about it. Six designers, each with one lens (logic, legal, engineering, everyday
life, the model's partner, and a free creative one), and two judges went into it. This is the one design that came out.

The idea in one breath: **before she asks anyone anything, Kas works out every sensible reading of your words, and every
answer your desk allows, exactly. If the answers agree, she just answers. If they differ, she finds the one thing that
decides it, and takes it to whoever can settle it.** That can be your records, which she reads herself; the maths, which
she works out; you, for anything that is yours to say; or the model, only for what your words meant, as a short numbered
question she checks before using.

You once said you could have been a power user thirty years ago with a library of books you knew how to call on quickly.
This pass is the half of that library that is *thinking* rather than reading.

## 2. Five things she can tell you about anything on your desk

**Why.** Right-click a card on the Field and choose "Why". For the finding "Startup is inside the Rev B limit of 40 ms"
she says: *does not hold. 45 ms is at most 40 ms: does not hold, 5 ms over.* Beneath that are its pieces:
- the 45 ms, from row 5 of the bench readings, reviewed from page 1 of the PDF;
- the 40 ms, from row 3 of Rev B;
- one choice still yours to make: that Rev B is the revision that applies.

The wires of that proof light up on the Field and everything else steps back. At the bottom: *"Rests on: 2 reviewed rows,
1 choice still yours to make. Model calls: 0."*

**What it rests on.** Take settled power, with your voltage good to a quarter volt either way: *"Holds: 2.9375 to 3.0625
watts against 4, with 0.9375 to 1.0625 to spare. Rests on your ±0.25 V, and on which revision applies."* Each of those has
a "Without it" button:
- without the tolerance, it holds with 1 W to spare;
- with Rev A instead, it holds with 31/16 to 33/16 W to spare.

Where you have set no tolerance, she says so once, plainly: *"With no tolerance recorded on the stopwatch, startup is 5 ms
over. A tolerance of 5 ms or more would make it cannot tell."*

**Either way.** This is the heart of the pass. When a question can mean more than one thing on your desk, she works out
each meaning and compares the answers.
- Settled power holds against Rev A's 5 W *and* against Rev B's 4 W, so she says *"holds either way"*, and nobody is asked
  anything.
- Startup, with a stopwatch good to 3 ms, does not hold under Rev B (2 to 8 ms over) but holds under Rev A (12 to 18 ms to
  spare). So she says *"it depends on one thing: which revision applies"*, and gives you two buttons with those answers
  already written on them.

**What would settle it.** Any "cannot tell" gets a short list of what would decide it. Say the startup current is known
only to within 100 mA either way and the voltage to a quarter volt. Startup power is then 9.4 to 12.25 W against a 12 W
ceiling: cannot tell. She says: *"Measure the startup current first. A reading settles this over 44/49 of its range; the
voltage, over half of its. These are shares of the range, not chances. With a meter good to 10 milliamps, a reading at or
below 969 mA settles it: holds."*

**Whose move it is.** Every open question is labelled with who can settle it: your records, the maths, you, a new reading,
or the model. Which revision applies is yours. A new measurement is the world's. "Next Friday", said today, which is a
Friday, is yours too: *"27 November or 4 December?"* The model is never asked what only you can know.

## 3. Your word

Much of what decides an answer is not a reading. It is something you say:
- which revision applies;
- what "Up to 40 ms" means;
- whether a bank statement is complete;
- your own account that a letter never came.

This pass makes all of these **one kind of record, "your word"**. It is yours to say, so she asks for it every time, at
every setting of the dial. When she suggests one, it shows as *"mine, not agreed"*, and the answer that counts is worked
out without it. If a reason behind your word later changes, the card is marked "reconsider". Nothing is undone behind your
back.

In this pass **she writes nothing herself.** Every new record is your press. Changes through her hand come in the united
pass after the next one.

## 4. The legal part: rules from your own sources

**The matter.** In the invented Home Demo matter:
- The Act's § 12 says a landlord must return the deposit, or give an itemised statement, within 21 days after the tenancy
  ends. That does not apply if the tenant gave no forwarding address in writing. A landlord who does not comply is liable
  for the deposit and an equal amount.
- § 3 says how to count: leave out the day of the event, and if the last day falls on a weekend or a public holiday, move
  to the next day that isn't one.
- The lease ended Wednesday 30 September 2026; the keys came back Saturday 3 October; the deposit was 1,200.00.

**A rule card.** You select § 12's words on the page and pin them. A pinned quote is the exact words, the page and the
file's fingerprint. Then you mark each piece as what the rule *needs*, what it excepts (*unless*) and what it *gives*. That
is a rule card, and each piece shows holds, does not hold or cannot tell.

**The last day.** Ask *"when is the last day for the deposit?"* She answers:
- **Wednesday 21 October**, if the tenancy ended with the lease;
- **Monday 26 October**, if it ended when the keys came back. Day 21 is a Saturday, and § 3 moves it to the Monday.

Both have passed. Then: *"which event ended the tenancy doesn't change the answer: nothing on your bank sheet shows
1,200.00 by either last day."* So she doesn't ask you about it.

**What would settle it.** Before you've said anything more, § 12(c)'s conditions *cannot be told yet*.
- Any one of these would settle it as "they do not hold": a credit of 1,200.00 by the last day; a statement you
  received; proof that no address was given.
- All three of these together would settle it as "they hold": your word that the bank sheet is complete; your account
  that no statement came; and something showing the address was given, which the agent's email of 29 September is.

**When it holds.** Once all three are in, the card shows § 12(c)'s *own* words, with 1,200.00 + 1,200.00 = 2,400.00
worked out. It begins *"On the rules you recorded, against the text you admitted"* and ends *"Not legal advice."*

**Absent is not false.** She never says "nothing was paid" because she found nothing. She says what she searched, 41 rows
from 1 September to 17 November, and that whether the sheet is complete is yours to say.

**The same machinery on Rail Demo.** It finds something no card says today. Rev B's 12 W startup ceiling is marked "Up to
40 ms", but startup lasts 45 ms, so for the last 5 ms no row on the desk gives any limit. She asks which applies there.
If it is the 4 W continuous limit: *does not hold, 10.8 W against 4 W, over by 6.8 W.* Comparing the cells of the two
revisions would never show you that.

## 5. The everyday part: money

*"How much can I spend each week on groceries and stay under the month?"* With 412.37 spent of 600.00 and 10 days left:
**up to 131.34 a week, 18.76 a day.** It is exactly 18.763, rounded down so that you stay under.

When a bill isn't known yet, she gives both ends and the turning point. With a household plan of 2,400.00 and the
electricity somewhere between your lowest and highest bills of the year, 84.10 to 142.55:
- *"Up to 24.00 a day whatever the bill is; 29.85 if it's as low as 84.10."*
- *"28 a day holds only if the bill is at most 102.60. At 142.55 you'd end the month 39.95 over."*

It is the same maths as the rail's, with the same receipt.

## 6. When she does ask the model

In this pass she asks the model one kind of question only: **which of these numbered things did your words mean?** Take
*"pop the bench readings' trace up"*. She understands "bench readings" and "trace", but not "pop … up".
- The model gets three options: *Trace it · Select it · None of these*. That is about 250 tokens, a token being roughly
  three-quarters of a word, and about five seconds on the small 4B model here.
- Before using the answer, she checks that your own words back it: "trace" and "bench readings" are in the same part of
  your sentence.

On answers the small models really gave earlier, the checker trims an extra goal the 4B added and refuses three goals the
2B picked for a request that named none.

**With no model loaded,** which is your ThinkPad today, the same three options are buttons. Either way she offers *"keep
'pop … up' as trace?"*, and next time there's no question at all. Your kept phrases are listed in her settings; you can
strike any of them, and none can ever give her permission to change anything.

The model is never asked:
- a fact, a number or a date;
- which revision applies;
- whether something exists;
- what a law means, or whether a claim holds.

In this pass it never sees a page of your documents, only card titles and your request. On your ThinkPad the 35B should
answer such a question in roughly two to four seconds. That is an estimate from its design, not a measurement; only your
machine can measure it.

## 7. The line, for legal and medical help

Everything she says in this pass is a template, a sentence written ahead of time; no model writes to you.
- A test reads every template and refuses words such as "you should", "entitled", "safe", "you have until", "likely" and
  "chance".
- How a word reads, which rule governs, and how days are counted where the source is silent are all your word, and they
  always ask.
- A rule must rest on words pinned from a page you admitted, so an invented law or case cannot exist on your desk.
- A date shows its counting rule, the day it was worked out, and the earliest candidate first.

Asked *"should I sue?"*, she says that is yours to decide and a lawyer can advise you, then gives everything she can
compute. Medicine gets the same pattern: *"I can't advise on medicines; your pharmacist or doctor can. Here is what your
records show."* Nothing medicine-specific is built in this pass. That page gets its own pass, after an independent safety
review.

## 8. How we'll know it works

Two measurements are written down before the work starts, so they can come out wrong.

**The everyday score.** Sixty requests on Rail Demo and Home Demo are each said three ways, plus a sealed set written by
someone who isn't building the dictionary.
- On the open set we predict she reads at least three in four exactly with no model, and **none wrongly**. A single wrong
  reading fails the build.
- On the sealed set we predict 55 to 70 percent, with at most one mistake, which will be named.

**The I05 experiment.** This is your idea from the specification. Its four arms stay exactly as written there, and two
are added: deduction with her short questions and checker, and no model at all. We predict:
- the no-model arm passes the three known cases in under ten seconds each;
- on a fourth case, sealed before its rules are written, it gives no wrong verdict and asks you one question rather than
  guess.

If it passes even that, the report will say plainly that this task doesn't need the model. If deduction fails with the
35B on your machine, it will say that the central bet was wrong.
