# An evening with Kas's mind

Kas 0.8.6, "Kas's mind, no model". Kas reads your words from the desk's own vocabulary, judges findings exactly
with the maths, says what would settle what she cannot tell, and asks a model only one small numbered question, or shows
you the same choices as buttons when no model is loaded. Everything here works with no model.

*Since 1.0:* you speak to her in her ask line, at the foot of her column ("ask Kas…", with its send arrow); words
typed in Line at the foot of the window are offered to her with Ask Kas →. Her column has no picture of her in any
look. The rest of the window is Help ▸ The Living Desktop; what she does beyond these ten things is Help ▸ Kas, the
Ghost in the Shell.

Two invented desks carry the examples. **Rail Demo** (a 12 V rail: bench readings, and limits in Rev A, archived, and
Rev B, current) is built by Help ▸ Try Rail Demo. **Home Demo** is an invented household: a tenancy under the *Demoland
Tenancy Act (invented)*, a bank sheet and two typed money sheets, built by Help ▸ Try Home Demo. Each is built through the
Kernel's own doors in a new folder of its own, never in your workspace; her card says so before anything is built, and the
demo opens on the Field in a window of its own. Every Home Demo legal page says INVENTED: NOT THE LAW OF ANY PLACE.
"Today" in every example is Friday 20 November 2026.

Ten things to try, one for each thing she can do.

## 1. Why, with the wires lit

Open Rail Demo (Help ▸ Try Rail Demo), right-click the finding "Startup is inside the Rev B limit of 40 ms" and choose
Why. Her column shows a short proof: 45 ms is at most 40 ms does not hold, 5 ms over; each line says its state and where
it came from, and clicking a line selects that card and lights the proof's wires in the Field while the rest of the desk
steps back. Next and Back walk the proof card by card from the source end; the last line says what would change it.

## 2. Ask in your own words

Type in her ask line, at the foot of her column: *How much current can the rail settle at and stay under the current
limit?*

Under it she marks each word with what it meant, greys the words she did not use, and says the request as she understood
it. No limit in amps is on the desk, so she reads "current limit" as Rev B's settled power limit, 4 W, and says that she
reached this by elimination. The answer: below 1/3 A, up to 333 mA in whole milliamps. Her card says *read from your words
· model calls: 0*.

On Home Demo, *How much can I spend each week on groceries and stay under the month?* gives up to 131.34 a week (18.76 a
day) for the 10 days left: 187.63 is left of 600.00.

## 3. What it rests on, and without it

Beside Why, Rests on lists your tolerances and your word on open points, such as which revision applies. Each has a
Without it button: settled power with your ±0.25 V tolerance holds with 0.9375 to 1.0625 W to spare; without the
tolerance, with 1 W to spare.

## 4. Either way

Ask *is startup ok?* Startup depends on one thing, which revision applies: under Rev B it does not hold, 5 ms over; under
Rev A it holds, 15 ms to spare. Settled power holds either way.

## 5. What would settle it

On a cannot tell, the What would settle it list says who can settle each thing: your records, the maths, you, a new
reading, or the model. Which revision applies is always yours.

## 6. Room to spare

Each goal shows the room left at its worst case, as a share of the limit, never of the reading. Settled power holds with
15/16 W to spare under Rev B.

## 7. Rules from your own sources

On Home Demo, pin § 12(a), (b) and (c) of the Act and mark them as needs, unless and gives. The rule card asks whether
§ 12(c)'s conditions, as you recorded them, hold on your records. It is your reasoning over your records, not legal advice.

## 8. Absent is not false

Nothing on "Home Demo bank Sep–Nov" shows a credit of 1,200.00. Whether none was made rests on that sheet being complete,
which is yours to say.

## 9. Checked against the desk

On Rail Demo, set the settled current to 300 mA (the demo's own change), then open "Rail Demo summary". Its margin says
*2 numbers differ from the desk*, and each number carries its mark:

- "250 mA" differs (row 3 now says 300 mA);
- "3 W" differs (settled power is now 3.6 W);
- "4 W", "45 ms", "60 ms" and "40 ms" are on the desk (so is "12 V");
- "5 ms" is made from the desk (45 ms less 40 ms).

A mark never says "true". The marks are on the desk, made from the desk, rounded, differs, unit slip, not on the desk and
not checked. A letter that says "the last day was 20 October" gets *20 October: not on the desk (the desk has
21 October or 26 October)*.

## 10. One small question, or buttons, and your words remembered

Type in her ask line *pop the bench readings' trace up*. She understands "bench readings" and "trace", but not
"pop … up".

- With no model loaded, the choices are buttons: *1 Trace “Rail Demo bench readings”: show where it comes from and what
  rests on it* · *2 Select “Rail Demo bench readings” in the Field* · *None of these*. Your press is your own approval.
- With a model loaded, the same choices are its one small question. Its answer is used only when it fits your words
  ("trace" and "bench readings" sit in the same clause); her card then says *The model's pick, checked against your words
  · model calls: 1*, and a fold shows what was asked, its size, the seconds, the model's own answer and her check.

After either, she offers once: *Keep “pop … up” as trace?* Keep it, and next time the same words are read as you kept them,
with no question and no model. A kept phrase that names a sheet with revisions either follows the current revision (when a
newer one arrives and is marked current, the phrase moves to it and says so) or stays on the one you kept; you choose
which. A phrase never makes a change. A phrase you strike is never offered again, and forgetting one leaves only a
tombstone: the day it was forgotten, never its words.
