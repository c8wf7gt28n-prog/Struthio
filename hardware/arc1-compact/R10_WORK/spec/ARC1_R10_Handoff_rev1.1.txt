2. System interfaces requiring schematic changes
USB topology: two USB-A ports plus recovery
The existing R10 allocation uses F133 USB1 host for the internal F133 to D213 link. Do not reassign it without a verified substitute. The remaining F133 USB0 dual-role connection must provide both USB-C device/recovery and host connectivity to TWO external USB-A connectors. Design and validate the additional hub/mux/control circuitry required; the two external USB-A ports cannot be treated as two independent USB controllers.
Candidate architecture for verification: F133 USB0  to  USB 2.0 role/data switch  to  (a) USB-C device/recovery path OR (b) internal USB 2.0 hub serving both USB-A receptacles. Verify upstream role, high-speed operation, reset state, VBUS controls and signal integrity.
In normal host mode, both USB-A receptacles shall function concurrently for Player 1 and Player 2. In USB-C computer-recovery mode, both USB-A host ports may be intentionally disabled. Make that mutual exclusion explicit.
Recovery should be accessible without a user-facing button: use an electrically deterministic power-up policy, firmware-independent ROM entry method if supported, and internal service pads or a fixture for forced recovery. Do not assume USB ROM behavior without bench evidence.
Provide USB-A 5 V host supply switches, overcurrent protection, port power budgeting, connector ESD, and recovery-mode isolation. Do not backfeed USB-C or a connected computer.
HDMI with actual audio
The previously selected TI TFP410 is a video/DVI transmitter; it cannot embed HDMI audio. Replace it with a verified, sourceable HDMI audio-capable transmitter or equivalent proven solution. Confirm D213 parallel video compatibility, timing, I²S stereo audio input and clocks, DDC/EDID, HPD, 5 V HDMI supply/limits, ESD and initialization software.
Target baseline: 1280×720 at 60 Hz video, stereo PCM audio (48 kHz baseline). Verify exact source clocking and supported audio sample formats in the selected transmitter datasheet.
No external audio jack and no separate audio cable. HDMI audio must work with an ordinary TV. Do not mark the schematic complete while the transmitter is only a placeholder.
Power with no physical button
Auto-start on valid USB-C power application. The power tree must use deterministic, safe sequencing for the F133, D213, both ECP5s, external memories and HDMI circuitry. If a software Stop/Shutdown command is provided, it may enter a safe idle or standby state; full power-off occurs when USB-C is unplugged. Reconnection must always produce a well-defined cold boot.
Check total worst-case load, including BOTH USB-A VBUS outputs. Use USB-C current advertisement/detection and/or USB PD according to verified power needs; do not assume a PC data port can supply 15 W.
Keep the USB-C recovery path electrically safe during restricted-power programming. Protect against abrupt user unplugging during storage updates.

3. Game-cartridge experience and internal eMMC
The front Player 2 USB-A socket doubles as a controller input and removable game-install source. The thumb drive is an installer/transfer medium; installed games run from ARC-1’s internal nonvolatile eMMC storage after import. This newly requested eMMC is separate from the existing 64 MiB system NOR boot flash and the four distributed working-RAM banks.

IMPORTANT IMPLEMENTATION GATE  eMMC is a NEW board requirement. Verify an available F133 storage interface, pinmux conflicts, voltage and timing compatibility, capacity, footprint and purchasing status. Do not silently substitute the earlier internal microSD design, or assume eMMC wiring exists in the generated schematic. Flag any incompatibility before routing.
Storage software shall specify a reproducible on-disk package format, game manifest, minimum version checks, finite capacity handling, file integrity, interrupted-copy recovery, write flushing and explicit “safe to remove” feedback over HDMI. The loader must work without a keyboard, touchscreen, or case-mounted buttons: navigation is through USB game controllers.
4. Case/PCB mechanical constraints
Plan a compact circular, low-profile shell with a smooth top. Place HDMI and USB-C on the rear face; place BOTH USB-A receptacles on the front face. The circle diameter is to be determined by the verified board, connector, thermal and mounting envelopes—not fixed at seven inches.
Reinforce the front Player 2/game-load USB-A connector for frequent thumb-drive insertion and side loads. Verify both front connector orientations, grip spacing, user-facing insertion clearance, PCB-to-case height, shielding and connector mating depth.
Reserve internal space for thermal conduction/air circulation, mounting bosses, appropriate PCB support, internal debug fixture access and any required HDMI shielding. No additional exterior opening is permitted for a service connector. Allow the circular outside diameter to grow or shrink as engineering requires.
PCB copper-layer count must follow routing/impedance requirements, not the 13 schematic sheets. Obtain an actual fabrication and assembly quote after native KiCad PCB design.

Non-negotiable go/no-go checks
The two external USB-A receptacles support simultaneous USB host enumeration in normal operation; internal F133→D213 USB remains functional.
The USB-C programming pathway has a demonstrable or credibly tested firmware-independent recovery mechanism without a physical button.
Audio is embedded into the HDMI signal and validated in the transmitter/data-path design; TFP410-only video is not acceptable.
An exact eMMC part and storage wiring have been verified against the F133 package/pinmux; a software loader alone does not satisfy this requirement.
The compact circular case has exactly four exterior connectors and zero buttons/switches: USB-C and HDMI at the rear, and both USB-A sockets at the front. Its diameter is engineered, not predetermined.
Unverified claims and first-article hardware tests are explicitly labeled OPEN. Do not describe the PCB as fabrication-ready merely because host tests pass.
