Small offices and the new AI assistants

More small offices now use an AI assistant to sort email, draft replies and summarise long documents. The
owners we spoke to said the time saved is real, but so are the mistakes. One bookkeeper found that her
assistant had summarised a supplier's statement and missed a late-payment fee in the footnotes.

Security researchers warn about a trick called prompt injection: a document can carry hidden text that tries
to steer the assistant reading it, for example by telling it to send files to a stranger. Their advice is
simple. An assistant should never send anything out on its own, and it should show the person what it read.

"Treat what the tool writes as a draft," said one accountant. "I check every figure against the paper."
